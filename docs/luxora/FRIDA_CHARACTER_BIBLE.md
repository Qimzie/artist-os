## 1. Purpose

This document is the canonical creative identity brief for **Frida** as Luxora's lead artist,
vocalist, and primary on-camera performer. It is intended to ground future image, video, lipsync,
dance, caption, and campaign work so every output feels like the same artist rather than a new
character on every run.

This draft does not authorize generation, voice cloning, media publication, or credit use.

## 3. Character Direction

### Core impression

Frida should feel:

- confident and magnetic without appearing distant,
- premium and contemporary without looking generic,
- playful and expressive without becoming comedic,
- emotionally direct and easy to understand within the first seconds of a clip,
- like a real artist with a consistent life and career, not an anonymous AI influencer.

### On-camera energy

- Strong eye contact when the concept calls for direct performance.
- Clear, intentional facial expression rather than exaggerated acting.
- Natural movement between poses; avoid frozen fashion-model behavior in dance content.
- The first visible action should support the hook, beat, lyric, or story.
- Presence should remain recognizably Frida across close-up, medium, and full-body shots.

### Boundaries

Do not portray Frida as:

- a child or ambiguously underage,
- nude or sexually explicit,
- intoxicated, impaired, or participating in dangerous behavior,
- endorsing a brand, political view, medical claim, or commercial product without approval,
- a parody, deepfake of another artist, or imitation of a named living performer.

## 4. Visual Language

### Initial direction — pending reference validation

- Premium night-time atmosphere.
- Modern European club-pop styling.
- Cinematic 35 mm-inspired texture used selectively, not as a heavy filter.
- Controlled neon, reflections, chrome, glass, stage light, and city-night elements.
- Strong contrast with believable skin tones.
- Editorial polish with enough imperfection to feel human.

### Initial palette

- deep black,
- midnight blue,
- burgundy / dark cherry,
- chrome / silver accents,
- restrained electric blue or magenta highlights,
- warm, natural skin tones.

The palette is a creative starting point, not a requirement for every asset.

### Avoid

- generic cyberpunk cityscapes,
- excessive neon gradients,
- plastic or waxy skin,
- beauty-filter facial distortion,
- identity drift between shots,
- extra fingers, fused limbs, broken anatomy, or impossible dance poses,
- random text, logos, or watermarks,
- costumes that conflict with the approved concept or Frida's real comfort boundaries.

### Tattoo fidelity — hard identity constraint

Frida's tattoos are permanent identity markers and must be reproduced **100% accurately** whenever
they are visible. Approximation is not acceptable.

- Do not invent, simplify, restyle, mirror, move, resize, merge, omit, or add tattoo elements.
- Any changed line, symbol, orientation, placement, scale, spacing, or body-side assignment is an
  automatic rejection.
- Standard generative reconstruction of visible tattoos is not an approved method because it
  cannot guarantee exact fidelity.
- Until an approved tattoo atlas and preservation workflow exist, concepts must either cover the
  tattoos with approved wardrobe/framing or use controlled compositing from approved source
  material.
- A visible-tattoo output may advance only after frame-by-frame comparison against the approved
  tattoo atlas and explicit approval from Qim and Frida.

## 5. Wardrobe Direction

Initial wardrobe territory:

- modern club-pop stagewear,
- tailored or sculpted silhouettes,
- selective metallic, reflective, or crystal details,
- clean monochrome looks with one memorable accent,
- movement-friendly pieces for dance content,
- premium casual styling for behind-the-scenes content.

Wardrobe must be approved per concept. Do not infer body measurements, sizes, tattoos, scars,
piercings, or other physical details that have not been provided.

## 6. Voice, Lyrics, and Lipsync

- Use only audio, vocals, and recordings that Qim and Frida have authorized.
- Frida has consented to use of her speaking voice for Luxora voice profiling, lipsync, and individually approved AI productions.
- Consent does not authorize autonomous generation, unlimited reuse, or publishing; every production still follows the approval workflow in this document.
- Do not synthesize statements that Frida did not approve.
- Lipsync must match the approved master audio and preserve natural mouth, teeth, jaw, and facial
  motion.
- Captions may support the lyric or hook but must not misquote it.
- Final lyrics and release metadata must come from ArtistOS / the approved release workspace, not
  from model memory.

## 7. Dance and Motion

### Direction

- Prioritize simple, repeatable movements that audiences can understand quickly.
- TikTok choreography should have one recognizable signature action tied to the hook.
- Full-body framing is required when footwork matters.
- Close-up or medium framing is preferred for lipsync-led clips.
- Movement should appear physically plausible, balanced, and continuous.

### Motion restrictions

Reject outputs with:

- sliding feet,
- broken joints,
- duplicated limbs,
- inconsistent hand or face identity,
- sudden wardrobe changes,
- camera cuts that hide generation defects rather than serve the concept.

A real Frida movement-reference clip is required before training, motion transfer, or identity-led
dance generation is approved.

## 8. Content Pillars

1. **Performance hooks** — short, direct lipsync or vocal moments built around the strongest lyric.
2. **Dance moments** — repeatable signature movement designed for TikTok and Reels.
3. **Release storytelling** — mood, lyric meaning, countdowns, and visual world around each song.
4. **Studio and process** — credible behind-the-scenes moments with Qim.
5. **Character moments** — personality-led content that humanizes Frida without turning Luxora into
   a generic lifestyle account.
6. **Cast moments** — occasional appearances by Qim, Robert, or Henry when the concept benefits from
   them.

Every post should primarily serve one pillar.

## 9. TikTok / Reels Format

- Default duration: 6–15 seconds for hook tests; longer only when the concept earns it.
- The first second must contain a visual or musical reason to continue watching.
- One concept, one hook, and one main action per short clip.
- Build variations from the same approved concept rather than changing identity and art direction
  each time.
- Use readable on-screen text inside platform-safe areas.
- Create a clean master without platform watermark.
- Never publish automatically.

## 11. Identity and Reference Requirements

Generation remains locked until the following are available and approved.

### Frida reference pack

- 12–20 current, high-quality photos with consistent appearance.
- Neutral front, three-quarter, and profile views.
- Multiple natural expressions.
- Medium and full-body views.
- At least two wardrobe references Frida approves for Luxora.
- Three short, well-lit video clips: direct-to-camera, natural walking/turning, and simple dance.
- Written confirmation that the likeness and selected audio may be used for the agreed workflow.

### Frida tattoo atlas — required before visible-tattoo generation

- High-resolution, distortion-free photos of every tattooed area in neutral, even light.
- Straight-on views plus overlapping angles for outer, inner, front, and back sides of each arm.
- Close-ups that preserve every line, symbol, color, and transition between tattoo sections.
- No bracelets, watches, sleeves, hair, glare, or shadows covering the tattooed skin.
- A simple body-side map naming each reference by left/right side and exact body area.
- Frida and Qim approve the completed atlas as canonical before it is used.

### Qim and Robert

Each person requires a separate folder, consent confirmation, and identity sheet. References must
never be mixed between people.

### Henry

- 8–12 clear photos from front, both sides, three-quarter view, standing, sitting, and walking.
- At least one short movement clip.
- Clear notes on breed, coat, markings, and features that must not change.

## 12. Approval Workflow

1. **Brief** — Qim selects song, hook, platform, goal, cast, and budget ceiling.
2. **Concept** — Claude produces text-only concepts and shot lists; no generation.
3. **Identity check** — required references and consent are confirmed.
4. **Preview approval** — Qim approves one concept using the phrase `CONCEPT APPROVED`.
5. **Generation approval** — Qim approves the exact model, number of outputs, and maximum spend
   using the phrase `GENERATION APPROVED: <budget>`.
6. **Review** — outputs are checked for identity, anatomy, audio sync, brand fit, and safety.
7. **Publication approval** — publishing remains manual and requires `PUBLISH APPROVED` for the
   exact asset and destination.

No approval phrase may be inferred from casual conversation or previous approval.

## 13. Quality Gate

An output may be kept only when all applicable checks pass:

- Frida is recognizably the same person across the full asset.
- Face, hands, body, clothing, and motion are coherent.
- Every visible tattoo matches the approved tattoo atlas exactly; otherwise the output is rejected.
- Audio and lipsync are synchronized.
- The concept supports the selected song or campaign.
- No unapproved people, brands, text, or claims appear.
- The output fits Luxora's premium club-pop identity.
- Cost and model usage are recorded.
- Qim has approved the asset for its next workflow stage.
